# Signal Roasters — Brand Foundation

Grounded in: [`signal-roasters-research.md`](signal-roasters-research.md)

## Positioning

Signal Roasters ships small-batch beans roasted within 48 hours of a delivery window timed to when you actually run out, not a fixed calendar date — for remote workers whose 9am cup is a start-of-shift trigger, not a treat.

Every researched competitor — Trade Coffee, Atlas Coffee Club, Bean Box — competes on variety, curation, and unboxing presentation, and all of them ship on a fixed interval, leaving the customer to manually skip or delay when the timing is off. Signal treats consumption-matched timing as the core mechanic, not a support workaround.

## Audience

A remote worker who has run out of coffee mid-morning on a workday and found that more disruptive than a missed package. They don't want to curate flavors or discover new origins — they want the cup to reliably be there when the workday starts, on a schedule that adjusts to how fast they actually go through a bag.

## Tone

Plain, operational, a little dry — reads like equipment documentation or a status readout, not a love letter to coffee. States facts (roast date, delivery window, days remaining) instead of adjectives.

**Never sounds like:** cozy, artisanal-with-a-story, apologetic about being a subscription, or excited about surprise and discovery.

## Visual Pillars

1. Product photographed like equipment on a plain surface — flat even lighting, no lifestyle staging, no hands-wrapped-around-mug shots, no steam-and-window-light.
2. Palette stays desaturated and utilitarian (charcoal, bone, one signal accent color) — no warm golden-hour coffee-shop tones and no travel/postcard color palettes.
3. Typography is a monospace or grotesk workhorse face, never a script or serif associated with "craft" branding.
4. Every image includes a legible timing/schedule element (roast date, days-remaining countdown, delivery window) somewhere in frame — the mechanism is always visible, not implied.
5. Composition is grid-based and symmetrical, like a spec sheet or dashboard, never an artfully-scattered flat-lay with props.

## Avoid

- Lifestyle photography — blankets, sweaters, window light, someone gazing out a window with a mug, beach or hygge scenes.
- Unboxing-as-value theater — postcards, extravagant bag design, or any packaging flourish that competes with the product itself for attention.
- Seasonal gimmick flavors or copy referencing them (pumpkin spice, holiday blends).
- Merch upsells at checkout (mugs, tote bags, apparel) as a growth lever.
- Discovery/variety framing ("surprise you every month," "explore new origins") — the pitch is reliability, not exploration.
- Any claim of "ethically sourced" or "small-batch craft" without a specific, checkable fact behind it.

## Why this positioning holds

Research on Trade, Atlas, and Bean Box (see research file) shows the entire category sells discovery and presentation: quizzes and algorithms to surprise you (Trade), travel-themed single-origin postcards (Atlas), "packaging and extras" (Bean Box). Real subscriber complaints center on rigid schedules causing either a backlog or a mid-week outage, with the fix left to the customer as a manual skip/delay. That's the open lane Signal claims: timing as the product, not the packaging.
