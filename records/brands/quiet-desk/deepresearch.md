# Quiet Desk — Deep Research

Fast, single-pass market scan (competitors, category conventions, real customer language). All claims below are sourced inline; anything not sourced here does not appear as fact anywhere else in this brand foundation.

## Category snapshot: how "quiet keyboard" is sold today

The quiet/silent switch category already exists and is well-populated, but the entire category's proof standard is **subjective listening, not measurement**:

- **Click and Thock** (clickandthock.com), one of the most-used community sound-comparison resources, explicitly builds its whole method around controlled *audio recordings*, not numbers: "I test everything on the same keyboard setup—keeping the case, plate, keycaps, and recording conditions identical." Its tagline is "No talking, just the sound of Keyboards." The site contains **no decibel readings, no acoustic measurements, no technical sound specification of any kind** — the comparison is "listen to these two clips and judge for yourself." (Source: https://www.clickandthock.com/)
- Existing silent switch products (Cherry MX Silent Red/Black, Gateron Silent Red/Black/Yellow, ZealPC Zilent V2, ZealPC Healios) are marketed on **feel and dampening mechanism** — rubber/silicone bumpers, actuation-force numbers, factory lubrication quality — not on any published, comparable loudness metric. One aggregator did cite an isolated, uncredited figure ("a Cherry MX Silent Black tested in a soundproof chamber produced a mere 42 decibels") but this is a single unsourced number from a single switch, not a program of measurement across a product line, and no primary source or methodology is given. (Source: https://hirosarts.com/blog/the-quietest-keyboard-switches/ — flagged here as a single unverifiable data point, not treated as fact elsewhere in this foundation.)
- General guidance across the category does acknowledge that the switch alone isn't the whole story — case and mounting style affect final sound too — but this is offered as a caveat about *why silent switches disappoint people*, not as a second axis the seller measures or publishes. (Source: https://hirosarts.com/blog/the-quietest-keyboard-switches/)

**The gap:** nobody selling quiet switches at consumer scale is publishing standardized, comparable, per-product decibel numbers. The category's most trusted comparison tool (Click and Thock) treats "listen to the clip yourself" as the ceiling of rigor. A seller that actually measures and publishes dB output per switch, on a consistent rig, is doing something the current market leaders in "quiet" explicitly do not do.

## Competitors observed

- **NovelKeys** (novelkeys.com) — general-purpose custom keyboard retailer; sells switches, desk mats, and bundles ("Classic Bundle Switches," desk-mat collection) alongside keycaps, artisan pieces, and DIY kits. Positioning is broad hobbyist/enthusiast, not quiet-specific. No sound measurement claims found on-site. (Source: https://novelkeys.com/)
- **Divinikey** (divinikey.com) — LA-based custom keyboard store; self-describes as "the leading custom keyboard store to help build and source DIY keyboard kits, pre-builts, keycaps, switches, deskmats, stabilizers, and parts." Broad catalog, enthusiast/build-your-own framing, not acoustic-first. (Source: https://divinikey.com/)
- **KBDfans** — carries a dedicated "Pre-lubed Switches" collection, confirming pre-lubrication is already a purchasable convenience in the category, but it's positioned as a time-saver for builders, not as an acoustic-consistency guarantee (a hand-lubed switch and a factory pre-lubed switch aren't held to any published sound standard). (Source: https://kbdfans.com/collections/pre-lubed-switches)

Common thread across all three: they are **general keyboard-parts shops** organized around switch *feel* (linear/tactile/clicky), price tier, and build compatibility — sound is a filter tag or a marketing adjective ("silent," "quiet"), never a headline number. None ship on a recurring/subscription cadence built specifically around acoustic output.

## Real complaints about keyboard noise in shared spaces

Verbatim quotes from people describing loud typing as a real, current problem in shared work environments (Source: https://www.teamblind.com/post/people-who-use-loud-mechanical-keyboards-in-office-z6q0s5da):

- "My colleague uses a regular one like he's at his grand piano concerto performance." — Microsoft employee
- "Headphones don't help with clicking like that." — Tableau employee
- "Phone conversations = necessity. Loud keyboard = luxury" — CoreLogic employee
- "i switched to a quieter version when i no longer have my private office." — Airbnb employee

Counter-voices confirming the social-friction dynamic exists in both directions (people know it's a live issue, whether or not they personally accommodate it):
- "I love it. And my neighbors use it too or don't care." — Microsoft employee
- "I use one, because I have mad gamer skillz get.over it" — Northrop Grumman employee

This confirms the core tension Quiet Desk is built on: keyboard noise is a recognized, named source of shared-space friction, people do actively switch to quieter gear specifically because they lost a private space (echoing the "shared apartments / thin-walled offices" framing), and the people causing the friction often dismiss it rather than measure or fix it.

## Opening this supports

Because (a) the category's own trusted sound-comparison tool explicitly stops at subjective audio clips with zero decibel data, and (b) real complaints show people already reactively downgrade to "a quieter version" after losing private space rather than proactively choosing gear by measured output — there's a defensible, currently-unclaimed position: **sell the measurement, not the vibe.** A published dB number per switch, on a consistent test rig, every quarter, is a claim none of the observed competitors are making.
