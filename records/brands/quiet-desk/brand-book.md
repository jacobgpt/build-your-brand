# Quiet Desk — Brand Book

Grounded in: `deepresearch.md`, `avatar-sheet.md`, `offerbrief.md`, `necessary-beliefs.md`, `project-knowledge.md`.

## What this is

A quarterly subscription that ships a fresh mechanical keyboard switch set and a matched, pre-cut desk mat — combo, sized and dampened together — for people who type in a space they don't fully control the acoustics of. Every switch is selected and lubed for low sound output at full typing speed, ships already lubed (no DIY step), and comes with a published decibel measurement. Pricing is not yet set.

## Positioning

Quiet Desk ships a quarterly, pre-lubed switch set and matched desk mat selected and tuned for measured sound output first, with a published decibel number for every switch — not feel, price, or an unverified "quiet" marketing claim.

The category doesn't currently make this claim. Every general keyboard-parts shop we looked at — NovelKeys, Divinikey, KBDfans — organizes its catalog around feel, price, and build compatibility, and treats "quiet" as a filter tag, not a headline. And the category's own most-used, most-trusted sound-comparison resource, Click and Thock, is built entirely on subjective audio recordings: same rig, same conditions, "listen for yourself" — with zero decibel data anywhere on the site. That's the ceiling of rigor the market currently offers.

Quiet Desk is the one selling the number itself, as the product, every quarter.

## Audience

One real person: someone who types for a living, in a shared apartment or a thin-walled/shared office, who doesn't fully control the acoustics of their own workspace. They've either already been told their typing is loud, or they lost a private space that used to make it a non-issue — and switched to *something* quieter reactively, not because they were able to compare products with real data. They want to keep typing at full, normal speed. They are not trying to become a keyboard hobbyist who learns to lube switches to get there — that's exactly the step they want removed.

Real language from people living this: "My colleague uses a regular one like he's at his grand piano concerto performance." "Headphones don't help with clicking like that." "i switched to a quieter version when i no longer have my private office."

## Tone

Reads like a lab report or an equipment spec sheet — precise, plain, numeric. States the measurement instead of reaching for an adjective. It's confident without being sales-y: it doesn't need to hype "whisper quiet," because it can just say "38 dB @ 60 WPM" and let that be the argument.

It never sounds like gamer hype copy (unleash, dominate, RGB energy), never sounds like the soft, aspirational voice of a minimalist desk-aesthetic Instagram account, and never sounds apologetic or precious about being "just" a subscription.

## Visual pillars

1. Every asset that claims quiet shows the actual decibel figure in typographic focus — never an unquantified adjective like "whisper quiet" standing alone.
2. Palette stays in instrument/lab territory — matte black, neutral greys, one calibration accent (safety yellow or orange, the way test equipment marks its dials) — no RGB gradients, no soft pastel or beige lifestyle tones.
3. Typography is monospace or a technical grotesk, with spec-sheet numerals throughout. No display, script, or hand-lettered faces, anywhere.
4. Product photography treats switches and mats as measurement subjects: shot on a rig or plain surface with a scale/ruler reference, top-down or instrument-angle lighting. Never a staged flat-lay with plants, coffee, or warm ambient light.
5. Layouts read like dashboards or data sheets — tick marks, axis labels, waveform or dB-meter motifs. Never soft rounded cards, Instagram-square crops, or mood-board collage.
6. Any sound claim in copy states its test condition (switch name, WPM, distance from mic) the way a spec sheet cites test conditions — never a bare number floating with no context.

## What we refuse

- **Gamer-RGB aesthetic** — glossy renders, rainbow lighting, "unleash"/"dominate" hype copy, esports visual language.
- **Minimalist desk-setup Instagram aesthetic** — soft flat-lays, plants, coffee cups, warm window light, aspirational lifestyle staging.
- **Any "quiet" claim with no number behind it.** The category's own trusted comparison tool already stops at "listen for yourself." We don't stop there.
- **Hobbyist catalog framing** — feel-first language ("buttery," "thocky"), endless variant browsing, build-your-own-parts-shop energy. We're not a parts shop; we're a curated, measured product.
- **Presenting DIY lubing as a step the customer might take on.** The entire value of the product is that this step is already done.
- **Borrowed, unsourced decibel benchmarks.** Every number we publish is our own measurement, on our own rig. We don't cite someone else's uncredited figure.

## Necessary beliefs

For a customer to buy, they need to accept:

1. "Quiet" is a number, not a marketing word.
2. They can type at their normal, full speed and still be quiet — no need to type softer or slower.
3. A pre-lubed switch can be just as quiet as one they'd lube themselves — they're not giving anything up by skipping the DIY step.
4. The switch and the desk mat are one system, not two separate purchases.
5. Getting a new, measured-quiet setup every quarter is worth paying for on an ongoing basis.
6. The person they share walls with will actually notice the difference.

## What's confirmed vs. not yet

Confirmed: the sound-testing gap in the category (Click and Thock has zero dB data), that general keyboard shops sell switches/mats separately and organize by feel/price, that pre-lubed switches already exist as a convenience elsewhere, and that real people in shared spaces already name loud typing as friction. All sourced in `deepresearch.md`.

Not yet confirmed: pricing (not set), the actual decibel figures Quiet Desk will publish (no product has been tested yet — this is a brand foundation, not a tested line), and whether the audience skews more toward apartments or offices. Full detail in `project-knowledge.md`.
