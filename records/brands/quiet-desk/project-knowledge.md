# Quiet Desk — Project Knowledge (Synthesis)

Grounded in: `deepresearch.md`, `avatar-sheet.md`, `offerbrief.md`, `necessary-beliefs.md`.

## VERIFIED

- The category's most-used community sound-comparison resource (Click and Thock) tests switches by controlled audio recording only and publishes zero decibel measurements or acoustic specifications. (Source: https://www.clickandthock.com/)
- General keyboard-parts retailers (NovelKeys, Divinikey) sell switches and desk mats as separate catalog items, organized around build compatibility, price, and hobbyist appeal, not acoustic output. (Sources: https://novelkeys.com/, https://divinikey.com/)
- Pre-lubed switches already exist as a purchasable category convenience (e.g. KBDfans' dedicated pre-lubed collection), confirming pre-lubrication itself isn't a novel idea — but no observed shop pairs pre-lubrication with a published sound measurement or a recurring subscription model. (Source: https://kbdfans.com/collections/pre-lubed-switches)
- Real people in shared/office environments have publicly named loud keyboard typing as a specific, current source of friction, and at least one person reported switching to a quieter setup specifically after losing a private office. (Source: https://www.teamblind.com/post/people-who-use-loud-mechanical-keyboards-in-office-z6q0s5da)
- Quiet Desk's product mechanism (quarterly switch set + pre-cut, sized/dampened desk mat, switches selected and lubed for acoustic output first, pre-lubed with no DIY step, published decibel measurement per switch) is self-reported by the user about their own product — this is treated as fact about the offer itself, not an external claim requiring third-party proof.
- The user explicitly stated the brand refuses both a gamer-RGB aesthetic and a minimalist desk-setup Instagram aesthetic, and wants an acoustic-test-equipment feel instead. (Source: user input.)

## UNVERIFIED

- Pricing: not yet specified by the user — no number, tier, or model has been set. Do not use any price anywhere until the user provides one.
- The specific decibel figures Quiet Desk will actually publish for its switches — no real measurements exist yet since this is a brand foundation, not a tested product line.
- The single "42 decibels" figure found for a Cherry MX Silent Black in a soundproof chamber (from https://hirosarts.com/blog/the-quietest-keyboard-switches/) is an isolated, uncredited claim with no visible methodology or primary source — it is not used as a fact anywhere in this foundation and should not be cited as a benchmark without independent verification.
- Market size, subscriber demand, or willingness-to-pay for a keyboard-switch subscription specifically — no source found confirming this category (switch subscription boxes) currently exists at any scale; the deep-research search for existing quarterly switch subscription services returned no matches, meaning Quiet Desk may be first-to-category or simply hard to find via search — this is not confirmed either way.
- Whether Quiet Desk's target customer skews toward apartment/roommate situations or shared-office situations more heavily — the user's brief and the sourced complaints both cover both cases, but no data splits the two.
