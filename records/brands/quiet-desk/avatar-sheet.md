# Quiet Desk — Customer Avatar

Grounded in: `deepresearch.md`. Sourced lines are attributed; everything else is marked `(inferred, not sourced)`.

## Who they are

Someone who types for a living (or types a lot, daily) in a space they don't fully control the acoustics of — a shared apartment with roommates, or a shared/open office with thin walls or close neighbors. They are not a "custom keyboard hobbyist" first; they're a person trying to not be the reason someone else can't concentrate or sleep. `(inferred, not sourced — direct restatement of the user's own description of who this is for.)`

## Pains

- Their current keyboard noise is loud enough that other people have explicitly noticed and named it: "My colleague uses a regular one like he's at his grand piano concerto performance." (Source: `deepresearch.md`, Microsoft employee via teamblind.com)
- Headphones don't solve it for the people around them: "Headphones don't help with clicking like that." (Source: `deepresearch.md`, Tableau employee via teamblind.com)
- Losing a private space is what actually triggers the switch to quieter gear — it's reactive, not planned: "i switched to a quieter version when i no longer have my private office." (Source: `deepresearch.md`, Airbnb employee via teamblind.com)
- They can't currently tell, before buying, which switch is actually quiet versus just marketed as quiet — the category's own best-known comparison tool offers audio clips to listen to, not a number to compare `(inferred from deepresearch.md's finding that Click and Thock has zero decibel data — the absence of a measurable standard is itself a pain for someone trying to make a confident purchase decision)`.
- Manually lubing switches to get them quiet is a real time/skill barrier they don't want to deal with `(inferred, not sourced — direct restatement of the user's own description of the pre-lubed convenience they're solving for)`.

## Desires

- Wants to keep typing at full, normal speed — not type slower or lighter to manage noise `(inferred, not sourced — direct from the user's "low acoustic output at full typing speed" framing)`.
- Wants proof, not a marketing adjective, before they trust a switch is actually quiet `(inferred from deepresearch.md's finding that "quiet" claims in this category are universally unmeasured)`.
- Wants to not think about the social cost of their own workspace — to stop being the person named in a complaint like the ones above `(inferred, not sourced)`.

## Beliefs / objections

- Belief in the market: some people genuinely don't think keyboard noise is a real problem worth accommodating — "I use one, because I have mad gamer skillz get.over it." (Source: `deepresearch.md`, Northrop Grumman employee via teamblind.com) — this is the attitude Quiet Desk's customer is specifically *not* aligned with; their target customer already agrees noise is a real, valid thing to manage.
- Objection: "silent switches feel mushy / worse to type on than a regular switch" `(inferred, not sourced — a standard category objection about the feel tradeoff of dampened switches, not confirmed by a specific quote in deepresearch.md)`.
- Objection: "I'd have to become a keyboard hobbyist and learn to lube/build to get this right" `(inferred, not sourced — directly answered by the user's own pre-lubed positioning)`.
