“Quiet” is a number, not a marketing word.

The category's most-used sound-comparison resource builds its entire method on subjective audio clips — “No talking, just the sound of Keyboards” — and publishes zero decibel readings. “Quiet” stays an adjective you have to take on faith.

Quiet Desk publishes a decibel figure for every switch it ships, alongside the test condition it was measured under — switch, typing speed, distance from mic — the way a spec sheet cites its test conditions. Not an adjective. A number you can compare.

Low acoustic output at full typing speed — not just at a light touch. Every switch in the lineup is selected because it tests quiet at the speed you actually type at, not the speed a demo video types at.

You don't change how you type to keep the peace. The keyboard changes.
